\section{SYS-0}

The alphabet is listed as follows.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $V = \{v_i\}_{i \geq 0} \cup \{ A_i \}_{i \geq 0}$;
  \item $C$ is the set of constant symbols;
  \item $B = \{\Pi, \lambda, \Sigma, \cdots \}$ is the set of binders;
  \item $U = \{\mr{Type}_i\}_{i \geq 0}$ is the set of universes;
  \item $P = \{ (, ), : , \equiv \}$ is the set of punctuation.
\end{itemize}
All of these sets are at most countable. 


The whole formulas we use is contained by set
\[
  \mr F = V | C | U | B(V : \mr F)\mr F | (\mr F \mr F) | (C \mr F^*).
\]


\begin{notation}
  Here we announce some commonly used notations for avoiding ambiguity.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\bm a = a_0 a_1 \cdots a_m$;
    \item $f(\bm x) := f$ where $f \in \mr F$ and $\Fr f \subset \{x_0, \cdots, x_m\}$;
    \item $(f \bm x)$ refers $ (\cdots ((f x_0) x_1) \cdots ) $, note that this may differ the notation $ (f x_0 x_1 \cdots x_m)$, which is equal to our first definition;
    \item for any $b \in B$, $b(\bm v : \bm A)N := b(v_0 : A_0)b(v_1 : A_1) \cdots b(v_m : A_m)N$;
    \item $\bm v : \bm A$ refers 
      \[
        \begin{array}{llll}
          v_0 : A_0  & v_1 : A_1 & \cdots & v_s : A_s;
        \end{array}
      \]
    \item $M \rightarrow N := \Pi(v : M)N$, in which $v \notin \Fr N$;
    \item to distinguish between terms in $\mr F$ and constants in $C$, we use a mono font for constants;
    \item $\cl U := \Type_{\aleph_0}$, and $M : \cl U$ implies there exists a $k < \aleph_0$ such that $M : \Type_k$.
    \item for any relation $\cl R \subset \mr F^2$, $\mr{Compatible} \cl R$ is a abbrivation of the statement: for all if $M \cl R N$, then for any $L, A, \phi_1, \cdots, \phi_s \in \mr F$, $b \in B$, $\cc c \in C$ and $v \in V$, $(ML) \cl R (NL)$, $(LM) \cl R (LN)$, $b(v : A)M \cl R b(v : A)N$ and 
    \[
      (\cc c \phi_1 \cdots \phi_k M \phi_{k+1} \cdots \phi_s) \cl R (\cc c \phi_1 \cdots \phi_k N \phi_{k+1} \cdots \phi_s)
    \]
  \end{itemize}
\end{notation}



\begin{definition}
  A definition $D$ has the form 
  \[
    \cc c (\bm v : \bm A) \equiv d (\bm v) : B,
  \]
  where $v \in V$, $\cc c \in C$ and all of $A, B, d(\bm v)$ are in $\mr F$.
\end{definition}



\begin{definition}
  A sequent has the form
  \[
    \begin{array}{lll}
      \bm D  &  \bm v : \bm A  &  M : A
    \end{array}
  \]
  where $M : A$ is called a judgement, and both $M$ and $A$ are in $\mr F$. The set of all sequents is denoted by $\mr{Sqt}$.
\end{definition}



\textbf{Legal judgements} consist a subset of $\mr F : \mr F$, which is derived from a series of \textbf{rules}. In our SYS-0, we first introduce the following rules, each of them can be viewed as a relation on $\mr{Sqt}^* \times \mr{Sqt}$. 


\subsubsection*{Sort}
If $m < n$, then 
\[
  \begin{array}{lll}
    \emty & \emty & \Type_m : \Type_n
  \end{array}
\]




\subsubsection*{Var}
If $v \in V \smallsetminus (\Gamma \cap V)$, then
\[
  \begin{array}{llll}
    \bm D & \Gamma & A : \cl U  & 
    \\
    \bm D & \Gamma & v : A & v : A
  \end{array}
\]



\subsubsection*{Weak}
If $v \in V \smallsetminus (\Gamma \cap V)$, then
\[
  \begin{array}{llll}
    \bm D & \Gamma & M : A & 
    \\ 
    \bm D & \Gamma & B : \cl U   &
    \\
    \bm D & \Gamma & v : B & M : A
  \end{array}
\]


\subsubsection*{Conv}
For an arbitrary equivalence relation $=_a \subset \mr F^2$, if $B =_a B'$, then
\[
  \begin{array}{lll}
    \bm D & \Gamma & M : B
    \\ 
    \bm D & \Gamma & B' : \cl U 
    \\
    \bm D & \Gamma & M : B'
  \end{array}
\]



\subsubsection*{Def}
If $D = \cc c (\bm v : \bm A) \equiv d(\bm v) : B$, in which $d(\bm v)$ can be an empty string $\emty$, then
\[
  \begin{array}{llll}
    \bm D & \Gamma & K : L  &
    \\ 
    \bm D & \Gamma & \bm v : \bm A & M : N 
    \\
    \bm D D & \Gamma & K:L &
  \end{array}
\]





\subsubsection*{Inst}
If $\cc c (\bm v : \bm A) \equiv d (\bm v) : B \in \bm D$, where $d$ may be an empty string, then 
\[
  \begin{array}{lll}
    \bm D & \Gamma & \Type_0 : \Type_1
    \\ 
    \bm D & \Gamma & \xi_0 : A \tfrac{\xi_0 \ \cdots \ \xi_m}{v_0 \ \cdots \ v_m}
    \\
          & \vdots &
    \\
    \bm D & \Gamma & \xi_m : A \tfrac{\xi_0 \ \cdots \ \xi_m}{v_0 \ \cdots \ v_m} 
    \\
    \bm D & \Gamma & (\cc c \bm{\xi}) : B \tfrac{\xi_0 \ \cdots \ \xi_m}{v_0 \ \cdots \ v_m} 
  \end{array}
\]




\subsubsection*{Compu}
\begin{definition}
  For any definition $\bm D$, $\rightarrow_{\bm D}$ is the samllest relation constants
  \[
    (\cc c \bm{\xi}) \rightarrow_{\bm D} d(\bm{\xi}),
  \]
  for all $\cc c \in \bm D$, $\bm{\xi} \in \mr F^*$, and satisfies Compatible $\rightarrow_{\bm D}$.
\end{definition}







\section{SYS-1}

我们通过引入新符号, 增加新规则和关系来增加新的项和类型, 这个过程大致遵循一个固定的工作流:
1. Formation: 引入新规则, 让系统在指定条件下可以导出新类型;
2. Constuction: 引入新规则, 让系统在指定条件下可以导出新项, 并且这些项都具有新类型;
3. Elimination: 引入新规则, 告知系统新项应该如何使用;
4. Computaiton: 引入$\mr F$上的新关系, 告知系统新系统中某些表达和旧系统中的某些表达具有同样的语义.


现在我们将拓展SYS-0, 增加$\lambda$-Terms 和 $\Pi$-Types.

定义Elim前先明确需求, 对任何 Type 或者 Universe $Q$, 通过给定的项$\phi: \Pi(v : A)(B v \rightarrow Q)$, 其中$B v$指的是公式的合并$(B v)$, 我们可以构造出一个具有类型$(\Sigma (v : A) Bv) \rightarrow Q$的项 $\psi$. 所以, 有理由将规则设计成下面这个样子:
\[
  \begin{array}{lll}
    \bm D & \Gamma & \Sigma (v : A) B : \cl U 
    \\ 
    \bm D & \Gamma & \phi : \Pi (v : A) (Bv \rightarrow Q)

    \bm D & \Gamma & (\cc{Elim} Q \phi) :  (\Sigma (v : A) Bv) \rightarrow Q
  \end{array}

然后再加入新关系$\rightarrow_2$, 其包含下面内容
\[
  ((\cc{Elim} Q \phi) (\cc{pair} a b )) \rightarrow_2 ((\phi a) b)
\]


除此之外还有一种更普遍的方法, 它是从更复杂的功能中下放到这里的.

  \begin{array}{lll}

 \end{gathered}

  \end{array}

可见如果给出常函数$H = \lambda(X : \Sigma(v_0 : A)B)Q : \Pi (X:(\Sigma(v_0 : A)B) \rightarrow \cl U $, 还有

  \phi : \Pi (v_0 : A)(v_1 : B v_0) H (\cc{pair} v_0 v_1) =  \Pi (v_0 : A)(v_1 : B v_0) Q,

那么

  (\cc{Ind}\Sigma (v : A) B) (H \phi) : (\Sigma (v : A) Bv) \rightarrow Q.

    (\cc{Ind}\bb N)(P p_0 p_S) : \Pi (v : \bb N)(P v),
  \]
  namely, the proposition $\forall n : \bb N \ P (n)$ is true.
\end{example}
